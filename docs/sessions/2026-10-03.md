# 1.14: flavours with every colour, Drop 4, ghost notes, the bass lying back, the galant schemata in minor, the truck driver

[Decision 0026](../decisions/0026-flavours-ghosts-bass-pull-minor-schemata.md).

- **Flavours** show with every colour. With Triads the diminished seventh a
  third up is left out (it is a seventh chord); with Sevenths the existing
  rules already kept only the flavours that keep the seventh or trade it
  for a 6. Rates came out the same as Mixed's (160-162 of 300 Measures).
- **Drop 4**: one line in the voicer (`DROPS`), outside Any.
- **Ghost notes**: a Drums row (Off / Rare / Common). The places are the odd
  sixteenths (a shuffle's middle triplet), next to the backbeat likeliest;
  velocity 38 at any Velocity setting - the velocity rule in the sweep had
  to learn that. 1.5 a bar on Rare, 3.4 on Common. The demo marks them (g).
- **The bass lying back**: a hidden setting, half and half, as the user's
  "fewer buttons" asked in 1.13. `I.pullBass` works on the finished bass
  part. The sweep's first run found the bass changing a sixteenth early
  where it already moved inside the pulled eighth (the 3+3+3+3+2+2 rhythm):
  now anything inside the eighth goes and the new note comes where the
  chord does.
- **Galant schemata in minor**: Open Music Theory says most "can be
  converted directly to minor mode". The Romanesca's bass falls on the
  natural minor's te (a raised ti falling to le would be an augmented
  second); the Fonte cannot go ii-I in minor (ii is diminished), so it goes
  iv-III: told to the user rather than asked, as a sensible default.
- **The truck driver**: where no section after half way starts on the
  tonic, the latest that can is made to. 360 of 600 Measures changed gear
  in 1.13; 553 now. The rest end on a section that is only its close.

Found on the way:

- A Loop of a named progression drew its flavours afresh the second time
  round (its repeats carry the progression on rather than copy): a repeat
  that reaches the same point of the progression as an earlier unit now
  copies that unit (`twin`). Only showed once Triads took flavours, since the
  tests' Loops were in Triads.
- A Period's answer could open differently from its question where the
  copied chord was flavoured but the copy was a cadence's (which is never
  flavoured): such a chord is now not flavoured.
- The truck driver's V could be flavoured (A7sus4): it no longer is.
- The second deep sweep: a Monte's D7 cut off from its G by the truck
  driver's V (where the V takes a short chord's place - two a bar in 3/4):
  now plain. A named test needed those exact settings to see it; the first
  version of the test missed the sabotage, which is how that was found.
- A ghost note met a triplet kick at 9 1/3, reached two ways (8 + 4/3 and
  4 + 16/3), which a table keyed by the number did not see as the same

# Later - 1.15: the Neapolitan, the augmented sixths, the passing diminished seventh

[Decision 0027](../decisions/0027-chromatic-chords.md).

Read first, in the sources' own words (the extracted texts in the
scratchpad): Hutchinson ch. 20 (the Neapolitan), 21 (augmented sixths, and
their lead-sheet names), 29.3-29.4 (voice-leading both); Open Music Theory's
"Chromatically altered subdominant chords" and "Harmonic syntax -
prolongation" (passing chords). They agree, so nothing was asked.

- No new rows: the Neapolitan and the augmented sixths come with Borrowed,
  the passing diminished seventh with Applied - each on dice of its own.
- First measure: on Rare only 13 Measures in 300 had a chromatic chord: a
  ii or IV straight before a close's V is less common than expected (the
  walk often goes I-V). Letting the tonic's second half take it (i N6 V,
  as the books show) and raising the chances gave about one in eight.
- The demo showed "F#dim7 G/B" and "Eb/G Edim7": the inversions, which run
  later, had moved a passing chord's neighbours off their roots; they are
  now held (`rootHeld`), as is the chord before a chromatic one.
- The tests found: a chromatic chord on a Period's answer's copied opening
  (now a copy only follows its original); an augmented sixth replacing the
  chord an applied V7 led to; the second voice taking the augmented sixth
  itself under fi (now a consonant note of the scale, and the sweep's
  on-the-chord rule allows that one case); Fr and Ger sixths with Triads
  (now N6 and It+6 only); Open voicing not spreading a chord with no fifth.
