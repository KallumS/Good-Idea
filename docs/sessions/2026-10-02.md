## What was done - 1.5

[Decision 0018](../decisions/0018-flavours-voicings-and-inversions.md).

- **Flavours** (Off / Rare, shown with Mixed): about one chord in five that
  may becomes a sus4 (7sus4), sus2, add2 (inside), add9 (on top), 9, 6, or
  the diminished seventh a third up (G7 to Bm7b5). All from the scale, each
  only where its interval is real (no Fsus4 in C). Never the first chord or
  a cadence's; never a borrowed one.
- **Voicing** (Close, Open, Drop 2, Drop 3, Drop 2 & 4, Shell, Rootless).
  "Drop 4" alone is the bottom voice dropped - in root position just a lower
  root - so Drop 2 & 4, the standard pairing, stands for it.
- **Inversions** (Off / Rare): first where the bass steps, second only
  cadential, passing or held, third only falling a step to the next chord's
  root or third. The bass part and a Phrase's bass note play them; the chord
  line writes C/E.
- All three are new settings at the end of the list; Off, Close and Off are
  1.4, and 1.0's ideas are unchanged with them so.

## What the tests found

- **Repeats drew new flavours.** A Period's answer could take Csus4 where
  its question had C, and its copied tune bent to fit: the Period and Song
  tests failed. Copied chords now remember their original (`orig`, then
  `sl.origin`), and take its flavour and inversion.
- **Chained third inversions.** A third inversion could set the next chord's
  bass on its own seventh, which then went nowhere: Am7/G G7/F Em7/D. Now it
  resolves only onto the next chord's root or third (V4/2 I6).
- **Voicings gave up quietly.** Under a low tune the space was too small and
  the engine fell back to close position, so Rootless played roots. Now a
  voicing reaches an octave over the tune first, and only then plays close.
- **Open under a Phrase.** With a floor at C3 to stay above the Phrase's own
  bass, Am7 and G7 could never open. The floor is now G2 and the bass note
  goes under the voicing (Close keeps its old placement).
- **add9 never turned up**: it has Mixed's own add9's notes and name, and
  was thrown out as "no change". What it changes is the voicing, the ninth
  on top; it is let through on that.
- The Loop test first compared borrowed chords, which are one occurrence
  only (0011) by design; it now turns Borrowed off to see flavours alone.
- My first "Close changed" sabotage changed nothing (it set a variable the
  Close path never reads) and so "missed"; redone properly, it bit. The low
  interval limit was missed outright - the test only voiced chords in the
  middle register - and has a low-register test now. That test then found a
  real case: a chord with no rootless shape (Bdim has no whole-tone ninth)
  fell back to close position, which could sit a third below C3. The
  fallback now sits above C3 where it can.

**Proving the new tests bite**: fifteen breakages, all caught (listed in
CLAUDE.md).

# Later - 1.6: inversions checked against the textbooks, and Common

## What was asked

"Could you check your knowledge on inversions using the internet please.
Also, anywhere we have an option called Rare could we add one for Common
too please?"

## What the search found

Searched the web (Open Music Theory, Puget Sound's *Music Theory for the
21st-Century Classroom*, Toby Rush, and pages quoting them; the textbook
sites themselves are blocked from this container, so the search results'
quotations were read instead). First inversions and V4/2 to I6 were right.
Three things were too loose:

- the cadential 6/4 must come at a cadence, before its V, on a stronger
  beat - 1.5 allowed any I before any V;
- passing and pedal 6/4s sit on weak beats between chords of the same
  function, a passing one walking the bass one way - 1.5 checked only the
  steps;
- a diminished triad is "usually found in first inversion": in root
  position its fifth is a tritone over the bass - 1.5 treated it like any
  chord.

## What was done

- The six-four rules as the books give them, with a beat weight
  (`I.weightAt`) that makes the first of each pair of bars the strong one.
  Second inversions in 400 C major Measures went from 72 to 23; what is left
  reads V I6/4 V, I IV6/4 I.
- Diminished triads considered three times in four, first inversion only:
  I vii6 I.
- Common beside Rare for Borrowed, Flavours and Inversions: borrowed in
  about two ideas in three, with a second chord half the time in an idea of
  eight chords or more; flavours on half the chords that may; inversions on
  three in five. The same draws decide both, so Common only adds.
- [Decision 0018](../decisions/0018-flavours-voicings-and-inversions.md)
  has a 1.6 section; 0011 notes Common.

## What the tests found

- Common inversions came out 1.6 times Rare, not the 1.8 first guessed:
  "never two running" caps them. The bar is now half as much again for all
  three.
- A command of mine hung on a stray `cat` waiting for input; stopped and
  rerun.

**Proving the new tests bite**: eight breakages - a cadential 6/4 anywhere,

three or four MIDI parts, and the notes say which.

What came out of it (the first section of READING.md):

- **Against the books**, each in more than one source: an inverted chord
  doubling its bass note (G/B doubles B - Hutchinson, Open Music Theory and
  Rimsky-Korsakov all name it); a seventh on a half cadence's V with Mixed;
  bass-to-chord spacing and the tune sitting inside the chords (the
  orchestration books' "planes"), not yet measured; sevenths not made to
  fall by step; no check for parallels between tune and bass; one velocity
  for every note.
- **To add**, most often suggested: applied (secondary) dominants; named
  progressions and galant schemata; deceptive and evaded cadences;
  accented non-chord tones; the hybrid theme forms; a Pedal chord style and
  the tune in thirds; dynamics by beat strength; a last-section key change.
