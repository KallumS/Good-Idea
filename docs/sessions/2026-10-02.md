## What was done - 1.5

[Decision 0018](../decisions/0018-flavours-voicings-and-inversions.md).

- **Flavours** (Off / Rare, shown with Mixed): about one chord in five that
  may becomes a sus4 (7sus4), sus2, add2 (inside), add9 (on top), 9, 6, or
  the diminished seventh a third up (G7 to Bm7b5). All from the scale, each
  only where its interval is real (no Fsus4 in C). Never the first chord or
  a cadence's; never a borrowed one.
- **Voicing** (Close, Open, Drop 2, Drop 3, Drop 2 & 4, Shell, Rootless).
  "Drop 4" alone is the bottom voice dropped - in root position just a lower
  root - so Drop 2 & 4, the standard pairing, stands for it.
- **Inversions** (Off / Rare): first where the bass steps, second only
  cadential, passing or held, third only falling a step to the next chord's
  root or third. The bass part and a Phrase's bass note play them; the chord
  line writes C/E.
- All three are new settings at the end of the list; Off, Close and Off are
  1.4, and 1.0's ideas are unchanged with them so.

## What the tests found

- **Repeats drew new flavours.** A Period's answer could take Csus4 where
  its question had C, and its copied tune bent to fit: the Period and Song
  tests failed. Copied chords now remember their original (`orig`, then
  `sl.origin`), and take its flavour and inversion.
- **Chained third inversions.** A third inversion could set the next chord's
  bass on its own seventh, which then went nowhere: Am7/G G7/F Em7/D. Now it
  resolves only onto the next chord's root or third (V4/2 I6).
- **Voicings gave up quietly.** Under a low tune the space was too small and
  the engine fell back to close position, so Rootless played roots. Now a
  voicing reaches an octave over the tune first, and only then plays close.
- **Open under a Phrase.** With a floor at C3 to stay above the Phrase's own
  bass, Am7 and G7 could never open. The floor is now G2 and the bass note
  goes under the voicing (Close keeps its old placement).
- **add9 never turned up**: it has Mixed's own add9's notes and name, and
  was thrown out as "no change". What it changes is the voicing, the ninth
  on top; it is let through on that.
- The Loop test first compared borrowed chords, which are one occurrence
  only (0011) by design; it now turns Borrowed off to see flavours alone.
- My first "Close changed" sabotage changed nothing (it set a variable the
  Close path never reads) and so "missed"; redone properly, it bit. The low
  interval limit was missed outright - the test only voiced chords in the
  middle register - and has a low-register test now. That test then found a
  real case: a chord with no rootless shape (Bdim has no whole-tone ninth)
  fell back to close position, which could sit a third below C3. The
  fallback now sits above C3 where it can.

# Later - 1.7: part-writing by the book, and shaped velocity

Asked: "Can you update Good Idea so that it's inline with the books - fix 1
to 5 then add everything they suggest adding. Assume the books are correct
except for things where the books disagree with each other, check with me on
each of those things."

Four disagreements were put to the user and answered: Hutchinson's doubling
rule (never double an inverted chord's bass, but for a diminished triad); a
half close's V a triad with Mixed, Sevenths keeping V7; the bass within an
octave and a fifth of the chords; the chords just under the tune, allowed to
overlap a step or two. Everything the books agree on was done without asking.
Decision [0019](../decisions/0019-part-writing-by-the-book.md).

- A new setting, **Part-writing: By the book / Free**, and a new velocity,
  **Shaped**, both the defaults. Free and Flat give 1.6 (and so 1.0) back.
- First attempt checked the tune against every bass note; then a different
  bass setting changed the tune (separate dice broke). Now the tune is
  checked against each chord's bass, and a walking bass's passing notes keep
  clear of the tune.
- The bass's octave: first try broke the walking bass's step into the chord
  (29 of 341) and left wide gaps; rewritten in two passes, the chord's note
  first (where the note before can still step into it), then the notes
  between.
- Sevenths fell by step less often by the book (74%) than free, because the
  chords' window now moves with the tune: the resolution note lay outside it
  one time in five. The window now stretches to take it: 96%.
- The doubling fix first broke the voicings' own shapes (Drop 2's four
  voices, Open's span, Rootless's no-root); it now replaces rather than
  drops (but in close position with four notes or more), keeps the top's
  span, and never gives a rootless voicing its root.
- A wrong measurement nearly misled: broken chords play one note at a time,
  so "the chords' lowest note" at a moment was an arpeggio's upper note;
  spacing is measured on block and pulse chords.

Numbers (200 Measures, block, Mixed): doubled bass 191/191 → 0; sevenths
falling a step 96%; bass crossing 14% → 0, wider than a twelfth 1.5%; the
chords within a fifth under the tune 20% → 95%; parallels 9.4% → 1.2% of
moves; half closes on a seventh with Mixed 90 → 0.

Eight deliberate breakages, all caught. `tests/check.lua` learnt
`GOOD_IDEA_SAY=1`, which prints passing checks with their numbers.

The deep sweep (22.4 million checks) found 28 parallels in 87,424 moves the
tune could not get out of: the fix may not move a unit's last note, and
two such notes stood either side of the parallel. Only a cadence's last note
needs to stay; the rule now says so, and the sweep passes.
A second deep sweep found 19 more, and a third 3: the tune boxed in (every
nearby note a tritone, a third repeat or out of range) - now it looks
further afield as a last resort, and a half close's or an open ending's
last note may move to another note of its chord.

# Later - 1.10: tension, and a second voice

The tune half of "add everything they suggest adding". The books agree on
these; nothing was asked. Decision
[0022](../decisions/0022-tension-and-a-second-voice.md).

- **Tension** (Off / Rare / Common, Melody step, Rare by default): the
  suspension (held over a change, falling a step), the appoggiatura (leapt
  up to on the beat, falling a step) and the anticipation (a close's last
  note an eighth early). By the book the chords leave out the note a
  dissonance falls to while it sounds.
- **Second voice** (Off / Thirds / Sixths): a part under the tune, on the
  chord on the beat, last so the other parts keep their channels.
- The demo now marks tension notes: (s), (a), (ant).
- Suspensions came out a tenth as often as appoggiaturas (11 to 95 in 300
  Measures): their spot - a step down into a chord change - is rarer. They
  now have a chance of their own when the spot comes (54 to 91).
- The first tests found a Loop leaning differently the second time round
  (2 in 15): a statement's first note has no note before it to lean from,
  so nothing was recorded, and the repeat decided afresh. Every note now
  records "no tension" when nothing can lean there.
- An inversion changed the tune with Free part-writing: the tension notes
  looked at the real bass. Free, they now look at the chord's root, as the
  rest of a free tune does.
- In 7/8 an anticipation could land on a beat (the beats are uneven); it
  must now fall off the beat, and the sweep says so.
- The rule that the chords leave out a suspension's resolution was first
  credited to Hutchinson; the sources' text shows it is Open Music Theory's
  ("Embellishing tones"), with an exception for the 9-8. Corrected, and the
  chords now keep the root under a 9-8 (61 of 66 times; the rest are strokes
  of two notes or none).
- Thirteen breakages, all caught (Tension Off first crashed the suite; a
  cleaner sabotage fails the 1.0 ideas and the voicing shapes).
- The deep sweep (23.8 million checks) found four cases in tens of
  thousands: a suspension falling onto a pulled chord's bass, and one a
  minor ninth over it (a Phrase's chords, pulled late, change bass under
  the resolution - the bass is now checked there too); a 9-8 suspension
  hiding parallel octaves (C held into Bb over a bass going C to Bb - a
  suspension does not hide parallels, so the notes are now checked in their
  new places); and, from 1.7, an inverted chord's replacement note put
  under G2, where the bass could not get beneath it.
